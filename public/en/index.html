The Problem

Equipment such as HVAC, water pumps, hot-water systems, refrigeration and electrical infrastructure incurs operating costs that are often hard to attribute to a specific machine from utility bills alone. Below are four possible pain points we hypothesise some operators face. They are hypotheses we need to validate with customers, not proven facts about every business.

Unexplained expenditure

Energy or water bills move, but it is unclear which equipment or which hours are responsible.

Possible pain point

Equipment reliability

Faults are often discovered only when equipment stops, and maintenance stays largely reactive.

Possible pain point

Disconnected information

Bills, equipment lists, maintenance logs and system data sit in different places and are rarely read together.

Possible pain point

Uncertain upgrade decisions

Without baseline data it is hard to know whether an automation or efficiency investment is worthwhile.

Our Approach

A simple four-stage process, from understanding the facility to verifying any agreed improvement.

  1. Existing data / equipment
  2. Analysis
  3. Findings
  4. Decisions

Illustrative diagram of the proposed process. It does not show live data or a deployed system.

1

Operational assessment

We understand the facility, key equipment, bills and how things run.

2

Targeted monitoring where justified

Actionable findings

Observations and improvement opportunities, ranked by the evidence available.

4

Evaluation and verification

Verification of agreed improvements against clear success criteria.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an existing BMS?

No. An existing BMS or meters can help but are not required. The starting point is a conversation about the data you already have, such as bills, equipment lists and maintenance logs.

Do you install monitoring equipment immediately?

No. We start with an assessment. Monitoring is proposed only where justified and agreed in writing. Any electrical work is assessed separately and carried out by qualified personnel.

Is the initial conversation free?

Yes. The initial suitability discussion is free of charge and carries no obligation. A technical assessment or pilot, if agreed, would have a cost defined up front.

What results can I expect?

We do not promise savings. A pilot may surface observations and improvement opportunities, or conclude there is little to improve. The outcome depends on the evidence available and the agreed scope.

Where is the pilot available?

Our focus is facilities in Albania. Availability by city or region will be confirmed in the initial conversation.
